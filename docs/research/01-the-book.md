# The Waakzaamheid in the book

Patrick O'Brian, *Desolation Island* (1978), the fifth Aubrey–Maturin novel. She is
fictional. Everything below is from the text unless it is marked **inferred**.
Quotations are kept to a word or two; the rest is paraphrase.

Text read: the copy at readfrom.net, pages 17–40
(<https://readfrom.net/patrick-o-brian/page,18,400961-book_5_-_desolation_island.html>
and the pages after it). Plot cross-checked against
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desolation_Island_(novel)>.

## What she is

| Point | What the book says | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rate | A Dutch seventy-four, a two-decker | ch. 5–7 |
| Guns | 32-pounders on the lower deck, 24-pounders on the upper deck. The Leopard's people reckon the Dutch 32s throw metal "half as heavy again" as their own 24s | ch. 6–7 |
| Men | About six hundred. Jack puts her at twice the Leopard's weight of metal | ch. 6 |
| Hull | "Broadsterned", Dutch-built, unmistakable in the glass | ch. 6 |
| Bow | Bluff bows, which shoulder the swell | ch. 7 |
| Colour | Black-hulled, seen at twenty yards in the last action | ch. 7 |
| Bow chasers | Her chasers fire "trained sharp forward" from **bridle-ports** in her bows | ch. 7 |
| Rig | Higher masts and "towering canvas" than the Leopard; studdingsails aloft and alow. Jack guesses her **pole topgallant masts** will not bear skysails | ch. 7 |
| Odd canvas | To gain in light airs she sets little triangular sails from her topgallant yardarms down to the cap, "like an inverted skyscraper" — storm staysails hung by their tacks, on the main and then the fore | ch. 7 |
| Captain | Never named. A tall, heavy man in a light-blue coat with brass buttons, smoking a short pipe; in a black coat on the last day | ch. 6–7 |
| Mission | Bound to reinforce "Van Daendels in the Spice Islands" (Java) | ch. 6 |

## Her colours

She first shows a **British blue ensign** as a ruse and answers the private signal with
nonsense. When that fails the blue ensign comes down and "her own colours" go up
(ch. 6). O'Brian never says what her own colours are. See `05-flags.md`.

## The chases

1. **The Nymph** (ch. 5, reported). She met the frigate Nymph in a rainstorm just north
   of the line. The Nymph outran her in a two-day chase, then lost her foremast.
2. **First meeting with the Leopard** (ch. 6). An exchange at dusk: Leopard's stern
   chasers against the Dutchman's bow chasers. Leopard takes a hole in the main course
   and loses some scroll-work from her figurehead. Jack escapes in the night behind a
   burning pitch-barrel decoy.
3. **Light airs** (ch. 7). She closes again, firing slow single shots from her upper
   tier. A night boat attack in a calm is cut up by grape.
4. **The last chase** (ch. 7). In the westerlies, after about a week out of sight, she
   comes out of a squall under three miles off. A day and a night running east before a
   west-north-west gale, the Dutchman dead astern. She opens with her bow chasers at
   about half a mile. Her shot hits the Leopard's mizzen top and stern-post and smashes
   the cabin deadlights. Leopard fires back with two brass 9-pounders from the great
   cabin, timed on the crests. One Leopard shot jams a Dutch port-lid open.
5. **Her loss** (ch. 7). A Leopard shot brings her **foremast and its sail down over the
   bows**. She slews broadside on to a breaking sea, goes over on her beam-ends,
   broached to, and is gone with all hands in a moment.

**Note for the game.** The book's sequence is the exact mirror of the planned chase
mode: a heavy ship chasing before a gale, chasers against chasers, and a mast shot
away forward that unbalances her so she broaches. The book does *not* say she swamped
through open lower-deck ports; it says no one could fight a lower tier in that sea, and
that one lid was jammed open.

## Date

**Inferred**: early 1811. Jack says Mauritius and Réunion have been "cleaned up"
(Mauritius surrendered on 3 December 1810), and Daendels is still in Java (Janssens
relieved him on 15 May 1811; the British landed on Java on 4 August 1811 and the Dutch
surrendered on 18 September). Wikipedia gives "about 1811".
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_de_France_(Mauritius)>,
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Willem_Janssens>,
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Java_(1811)>.

## The real Waakzaamheid

Not this ship. The real *Waakzaamheid* was a Dutch 24-gun corvette (1786), taken off the
Texel on 24 October 1798 by HMS Sirius, bought into the Royal Navy, cut to 20 guns and
sold in 1802. O'Brian borrowed the name.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_corvette_Waakzaamheid_(1786)>,
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_of_24_October_1798>.
