# Paint and decoration

## Short answer

The book says black-hulled, and the one good Dutch colour source of her date agrees: a
pen-and-wash drawing of the Batavian 74 *Jupiter* by C. Toussaint, 1800, shows the
whole hull dark grey-black with **no yellow bands**, ochre masts with black hoops,
and an ochre or gilt stern. The rest is filled from Dutch models and marked as such.

## Decoration — from the Washington herself

Two records of her own decoration exist: the draught, and a dockyard model of her made
in 1797, now at Greenwich (RMG SLR0591, <https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-66552>;
stern <https://collections.rmg.co.uk/media/443/474/f5800_004.jpg>, figurehead
<https://collections.rmg.co.uk/media/443/477/f5800_005.jpg>; Crown copyright, used
for reference only). A third, bare-wood model of the same hull is in the Rijksmuseum,
NG-MC-91 (<https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/nl/collectie/NG-MC-91>).

- **Stern.** Round tuck. A hollow counter with **two stern-chase ports** with lids.
  Lower tier: a closed screen of eight glazed lights between carved pilasters. Above
  it a carved balustrade, and behind that the upper tier is an **open stern walk**
  with a door and windows, arched lights at the corners. A tall carved taffrail with a
  seated warrior, trophies of arms, cannon and a lion; a carved giant (a Hercules
  figure) at each corner.
- **Quarter galleries.** Two tiers of three lights, a carved frieze between, an acanthus
  crest above, a pendant drop below.
- **Head.** Open head rails and gratings, carved trail boards with trophies, catheads
  with carved ends.
- **Figurehead.** A standing Roman warrior in a plumed helmet, sword in one hand and a
  shield raised in the other. The draught shows the same figure.
- **Name.** None on the stern of any model or the draught. The *Vrijheid* record notes
  that in 1785 the Admiralty was still to decide where a ship's name should go.

## Figurehead for the Waakzaamheid

She is fictional, so it is a choice. Period-true options:

1. **A crowned Dutch lion**, as on the *Vrijheid* (1782) and NG-MC-661 — the classic
   Dutch figurehead and readable at any range. Ochre and gilt. **Recommended.**
2. **A standing warrior**, as on the Washington (1797) and the *Chattam* model of 1801
   (NG-MC-1257) — the newer Batavian fashion. More accurate to her date; harder to
   read at sea.

## Her name

Not carved or painted on the stern — no Dutch model or draught of the period shows one.
